# Pathways

> **Who this is for:** Anyone arriving at the guide who wants a route through it tailored to their role.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10

Pathways are role-based entry points into transformAItion. Each one explains what AI adoption means for people in that role, the responsibilities and decisions that fall to them, the questions they should be asking, and a step-by-step reading route through the rest of the guide. Each also lists common pitfalls and describes what good looks like. Pick the pathway closest to your role. If you have more than one role, which is common in small organisations, read the pathways in the order suggested below.

## How pathways work

**They are routes, not silos.** Every pathway points into the same shared material: [foundations](../foundations/README.md), the six-stage [lifecycle](../lifecycle/README.md), [governance](../governance/README.md), [measurement](../measurement/README.md), [assessments](../../assessments/README.md), [toolkits](../../toolkits/README.md) and [tool guides](../tools/tool-guides/README.md). The pathway tells you which parts matter most for your role, and in what order.

**Each pathway follows the same structure:**

1. What AI adoption means for your role
2. Your key responsibilities and decisions
3. Questions you should be asking
4. Your reading route (step by step, with links)
5. Common pitfalls
6. What good looks like

**Everyone should read two things.** Whatever your role, read [capabilities and limitations](../foundations/capabilities-and-limitations.md) and your organisation's acceptable use policy (or the [template](../../toolkits/acceptable-use-policy.md) if you do not yet have one). Most failures of AI adoption trace back to misunderstanding what the tools can reliably do, or to unclear rules about data.

**Pathways depend on each other.** Adoption works when roles coordinate. Leaders set direction and risk appetite; managers redesign work; IT and data teams make it safe and possible; risk and compliance set guardrails; people and learning teams build skills and handle consultation; everyday users do the work and surface what helps and what does not. The [RACI template](../../toolkits/raci-template.md) helps you agree who does what.

## If you have several roles

In organisations with fewer than about 50 people, one person may be director, manager, IT lead and data protection lead at once. A practical order:

1. [Leadership](leadership.md): decide why you are adopting AI and what risks you will accept.
2. [Everyday users](everyday-users.md): understand safe use yourself.
3. [Risk and compliance](risk-and-compliance.md): cover data protection and contracts.
4. [IT and platform](it-and-platform.md): set up accounts and controls.
5. [Managers](managers.md): roll it out to your team.

The [organisational readiness](../../assessments/organisational-readiness.md) assessment is a good starting point at any size.
