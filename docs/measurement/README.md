# Measurement

> **Who this is for:** Anyone who needs to show whether AI tools are worth it: leaders, pilot leads, managers, finance and transformation teams.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10

This section helps you measure the value, quality and risk of AI tools credibly, so that decisions about investment, scaling and stopping are based on evidence rather than enthusiasm or vendor claims. It is relevant at every stage of the [adoption lifecycle](../lifecycle/README.md): framing hypotheses in Discover, setting success criteria in Assess and Select, measuring in Pilot, tracking in Scale and Operate.

## Principles in brief

1. **Start with the decision** the measurement will inform.
2. **Measure outcomes, not activity.** Usage and licence counts are leading indicators, not value.
3. **Take a baseline** before anything changes.
4. **Estimate the counterfactual.** Compare with a group without the tool wherever you can.
5. **Measure quality and risk alongside speed.**
6. **Treat self-reported savings with caution.** Research shows perceptions and measurements can differ substantially; see [evidence on impact](../foundations/evidence-on-impact.md).
7. **Be proportionate.** A small organisation trialling a low-cost tool needs a spreadsheet, not an evaluation team.
