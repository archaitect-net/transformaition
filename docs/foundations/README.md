# Foundations

> **Who this is for:** Everyone, whatever your role. Start here if you want a shared, evidence-based understanding before making decisions.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10

The foundations section gives you the common ground that every other part of the guide builds on: what AI tools are, what they can and cannot reliably do, what rigorous research says about their effects at work, and what the jargon means. It is deliberately vendor-neutral and cites its sources, so you can use it to brief colleagues, test claims made by suppliers, and set realistic expectations for a [pilot](../lifecycle/04-pilot.md) or [business case](../../toolkits/business-case-template.md).

## Five things to take away

1. **"AI tools" is not one thing.** A chat assistant, an embedded copilot and an autonomous agent have very different risk profiles. Classify tools by what they can access and what they can do.
2. **The capability boundary is uneven and invisible.** Tools can be excellent at one task and poor at a similar-looking one. Test on your own tasks.
3. **Fluency is not accuracy.** Verification is a permanent part of working with generative AI, not a temporary workaround.
4. **Task gains do not automatically become organisational gains.** Controlled studies show large effects on suitable tasks; effects on whole jobs and organisations are typically much smaller and depend on how work is redesigned.
5. **Who uses the tool matters.** Benefits vary by experience and skill, and can reverse when users cannot judge the output.
