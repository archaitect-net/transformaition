# Communication plan template

> **Who this is for:** Programme leads, internal communications, HR and managers responsible for explaining AI adoption to staff and other stakeholders. Includes a light version for small organisations.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10

This template helps you plan communication about AI adoption that is honest, two-way and timed to the lifecycle. It covers objectives, audience analysis, core messages (including how to talk about jobs and limitations), a channel and timing plan by lifecycle stage, feedback mechanisms, frequently asked questions, and how to measure whether communication is working. Guidance notes in blockquotes explain the choices; replace [bracketed placeholders]. Use it from the [Pilot](../docs/lifecycle/04-pilot.md) stage onwards, and especially when you [Scale](../docs/lifecycle/05-scale.md).

> **Guidance: principles**
>
> - **Be specific and honest.** Vague enthusiasm ("AI will transform everything") or vague reassurance ("nothing will change") both erode trust. Say what you know, what you don't, and when you will know more.
> - **Address the questions people actually have.** Common ones: Will this affect my job? Am I allowed to use it? What happens to my data? What if it gets something wrong? Who do I ask?
> - **Make it two-way.** Provide ways for people to ask questions, raise concerns and share what works, and show that you act on them.
> - **Managers are the main channel.** People tend to look to their line manager for what a change means for them. Equip managers before general announcements.
> - **Consult where required.** Significant changes to roles or working practices may require consultation with staff or their representatives under employment law or collective agreements. Take HR and legal advice.

## 3. Core messages

> **Guidance:** Draft three to five messages that everyone uses consistently. Test them with a few staff and managers before launch. Avoid claims you cannot support; if you mention benefits, base them on your own pilot data where possible.

| # | Message | Supporting points |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Why:** [We are introducing AI tools to help with [specific tasks] so that [specific benefit].] | [Link to objectives; what problem it solves] |
| 2 | **What it is and isn't:** [AI tools can help with drafting, summarising and searching. They can also be wrong, so you remain responsible for checking their output.] | [Link to [capabilities and limitations](../docs/foundations/capabilities-and-limitations.md)] |
| 3 | **Rules:** [Use approved tools only, follow the data rules, and check outputs.] | [Link to [acceptable use policy](acceptable-use-policy.md)] |
| 4 | **People:** [Our intentions for roles and jobs are [state honestly]. We will [consult / involve] you on changes that affect your role.] | [What has been decided; what has not; when you will know more] |
| 5 | **Help and voice:** [Training is available; here is where to ask questions or raise concerns, and here is how we will respond.] | [Channels; response commitments] |

> **Guidance on jobs:** Do not promise what you cannot guarantee. If leadership has decided that AI adoption will not lead to redundancies within a period, say so and say for how long. If no decision has been made, say that, explain how decisions will be made and how people will be involved. Silence is usually filled with rumour.

## Light version for small organisations

For a small team, a one-page plan is enough:

1. **Team meeting** before introducing tools: why, what's allowed, how to get help, honest answer on jobs.
2. **Written summary** afterwards: the one-page quick reference from the [acceptable use policy](acceptable-use-policy.md).
3. **Shared channel or regular slot** to swap tips and raise issues.
4. **Check-in after a month:** what's working, what isn't, what to change.
5. **Update everyone** when tools or rules change.
