# Videos

> **Who this is for:** Anyone who prefers to learn by watching, and trainers, champions and L&D teams looking for credible videos to include in AI literacy and adoption training. Contributors who want to suggest videos should read the inclusion criteria.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10

This section does two things. First, it maintains a small, curated catalogue of external videos that explain AI tools and their adoption accurately and neutrally, organised by pathway and lifecycle stage, with clear inclusion criteria so the catalogue stays trustworthy. Second, it hosts scripts for short videos this project plans to produce, so they can be reviewed in the open before recording. Every catalogue entry below has been checked to exist at the stated source; entries are dated because AI content ages quickly. The catalogue is deliberately short: we would rather list a few verified videos than many unverified ones.

## Why a curated catalogue

There is a great deal of video content about AI. Much of it is promotional, out of date, or overstates what tools can do. A short list of credible sources saves learners time and reduces the risk of training staff on inaccurate material. Videos complement, and do not replace, the written guidance: where a video and this guide disagree, check the cited sources and tell us.

## Inclusion criteria

A video is included only if it meets **all** of the following.

| Criterion | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Credible source** | Published by a standards body, regulator, government, public research institute, university, established public-interest science institution, or an independent educator with a track record of accurate explanation |
| **Accurate** | Content is consistent with primary sources and this guide's [foundations](../docs/foundations/README.md). Claims about performance or impact are qualified or evidenced |
| **Neutral** | Does not promote a specific product. Mentions of products are incidental or illustrative |
| **Dated** | Publication or recording date is known and shown in the catalogue |
| **Available** | Freely viewable without a paid account (registration-only is acceptable if noted) |
| **Accessible** | Captions available (auto-generated captions are acceptable but noted where known to be poor) |
| **Still relevant** | Core content remains valid. Older explainers of fundamentals may stay; content about specific products, prices or regulation dates is reviewed against current facts |

**Vendor videos.** Videos produced by AI vendors are not included in this catalogue. They may be linked from [tool guides](../docs/tools/tool-guides/README.md), where they are labelled as vendor material and used only to show how a chosen tool is configured or used.

**Speaker affiliations.** Many experts work for, advise or have worked for AI developers. That does not disqualify a video, but reviewers should check whether the content is promotional, and contributors should note material affiliations when proposing a video.

**Removal.** A video is removed or flagged if it becomes unavailable, is found to be materially inaccurate, or describes regulation or product facts that are no longer current without a clear date.

## Catalogue

**How to read the table.**

- **Stage** refers to the [adoption lifecycle](../docs/lifecycle/README.md): Discover, Assess, Select, Pilot, Scale, Operate. "All" means it is general background.
- **Length** is approximate. "(verify)" means the exact running time was not confirmed during review.
- **Reviewed** records when the entry was last checked and what was checked. "Metadata verified" means the title, source, date and link were confirmed; "content reviewed" means a reviewer has watched it against the inclusion criteria. Entries awaiting content review should be watched by a trainer before use in formal training.

## How to propose a video

1. Check it meets **all** the inclusion criteria above.
2. Open a pull request adding a row to the right table, or open an issue with:
   - title, source (channel and organisation), link, length, publication or recording date;
   - intended audience, pathway and lifecycle stage;
   - a two-sentence summary;
   - any affiliation of the speaker with an AI vendor;
   - confirmation that captions are available.
3. A reviewer will watch it, check accuracy against the [foundations](../docs/foundations/README.md) and primary sources, and update the "Reviewed" column.
4. Do not add links you have not opened yourself.

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for general standards.
