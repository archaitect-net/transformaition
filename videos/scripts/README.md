# Video scripts

> **Who this is for:** Contributors writing, reviewing or recording transformAItion videos, and trainers who want to adapt the scripts for their own organisation.
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10

This page sets out how scripts for transformAItion videos are written: the format every script follows, target length, writing style, accessibility requirements (including captions and transcripts), how claims are sourced, and the review process a script goes through before it is recorded. ## Format

Every script uses the same structure.

1. **Header block:** title, audience, length target, lifecycle stage, learning outcomes, status, and the standard "Who this is for / Last reviewed" lines.
2. **Scenes.** Each scene has:
   - a scene number, title and approximate duration;
   - **Visual:** what is on screen (presenter, animation, screen recording, diagram);
   - **On-screen text:** short captions, labels or key points (separate from subtitles);
   - **Narration:** the words spoken, written to be read aloud.
3. **Sources:** numbered list of sources for every factual claim, with scene references.
4. **Production notes:** accessibility, adaptation points, and items to re-check before recording.

## Length and pace

- **Target 5–8 minutes.** Short videos are easier to fit into training sessions and to update.
- **Plan narration length by timing it.** Our scripts assume a planning rate of roughly 130–150 words per minute for calm explanatory narration, so a 6-minute video has about 800–900 words. This is a working assumption, not a standard: time a natural read-through and adjust.
- **One idea per scene.** Most scenes are 30–60 seconds.

## Writing style

- Follow the guide's [editorial standards](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#editorial-standards): neutral, evidence-led, British English.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, active voice, concrete examples. Avoid reading out URLs or long numbers.
- **No vendor names** in narration or visuals, other than in a clearly labelled example where comparable alternatives are named too. Use generic screen mock-ups rather than real product interfaces.
- **Fictional examples** must be labelled as fictional on screen.
- **Any statistic or research finding** must appear in the sources list, and the narration must state the context (who was studied, on what task). Prefer "one study found…" to unqualified generalisations.
- Direct quotes must be under 15 words and attributed.

## Accessibility

Videos must be usable by people with a range of access needs. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) from the W3C set out requirements for time-based media, including captions for prerecorded audio and audio description or a media alternative for prerecorded video [1].

| Requirement | How we meet it |
|---|---|
| **Captions** | Accurate, human-checked closed captions in the published language. Auto-generated captions are not sufficient on their own |
| **Transcript** | A full text transcript published alongside each video. The script, updated to match the final recording, serves as the basis |
| **Visual information described** | Narration describes anything important that appears only visually (for example, a chart's key message), so a separate audio description track is not needed |
| **On-screen text** | High contrast; large enough to read on a phone; on screen long enough to read twice; not covering captions |
| **No flashing content** | Avoid rapid flashing or strobing |
| **Plain language** | Jargon defined on first use, consistent with the [glossary](../../docs/foundations/glossary.md) |
| **Audio** | Clear voice; background music low or absent |

## Review process

| Stage | Who | What they check |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Draft | Author | Format, length, learning outcomes, sources |
| 2. Accuracy review | Reviewer with subject knowledge | Every factual claim matches its source; nothing overstated; dates current |
| 3. Neutrality review | Second reviewer | No vendor preference; balanced framing; fictional examples labelled |
| 4. Accessibility review | Reviewer | Narration describes visuals; on-screen text legible; caption plan |
| 5. Pre-recording check | Author | Re-verify time-sensitive facts (regulation dates, research updates) within one month of recording |
| 6. Post-production check | Reviewer | Captions accurate; transcript matches; visuals match script |
| 7. Publish and date | Maintainer | Recording date shown; added to [catalogue](../README.md#videos-we-plan-to-produce) |

Changes are made by pull request so that the review history is visible. After publication, scripts are reviewed at least every 12 months, in line with the guide's [currency standard](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#3-currency).

## Adapting scripts for your organisation

You may record your own version. Typical adaptations:

- replace generic examples with ones from your work (keep them realistic and permitted under your policies);
- add your approved tools list, data rules and contacts in the relevant scenes;
- keep the sources list and update it if you add claims.
